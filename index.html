The rules of territory

Close the loop.
Claim the inside.

Connect tiles of your team’s color into a closed loop. When you close the last gap, the tiles inside become yours.

Requires Enclosure: ON in the match rules.

  1. 1

    Build a connected line

    Walk over normal tiles to paint them. Your line must connect along the six sides of the hexagons.

  2. 2

    Close the last gap

    Join the ends with your team’s color. A loop can be any shape; it does not have to be a perfect hexagon.

  3. 3

    Take the inside

    Enclosed neutral and enemy normal tiles, including obstacle floors, change to your team’s color immediately.

Work together. Tiles painted by teammates, CPU teammates, cannons or earlier enclosures can all be part of your loop.

What counts as a boundary?

A closed line of your team’s tiles matters more than the shape of the map.

Obstacle floors and cannons: the extra rules

You cannot paint an obstacle floor by walking over it. Cannon fire or an enclosure can take it. Once it is your color, it can help form another loop.

Taking a cannon can complete a loop immediately. A neutral or enemy-owned cannon tile does not count as your boundary. Enclosing a cannon does not capture the cannon itself, and its tile does not add to your territory count.

What if a hole, base or cannon is inside the loop?

You can still capture the eligible tiles around it. Special tiles and holes are skipped. Base and cannon ownership does not change.

A hole or the edge of the map cannot replace a missing part of your boundary. You still need a complete loop of your own tiles.

Bases & cannons

Stand on the capture point to take control. Enclosing the surrounding floor does not transfer ownership.

If teams mixAny non-owner team keeps capture progressing, even with defenders presentProgress resets while different teams are present

More players do not make the timer faster. Base attackers share the same progress. At completion, the non-owner team with the most players on the core takes it; ties use the fixed team order.

What does owning a base do?

Living teammates recover 10 HP per second on their owned base’s seven tiles, up to maximum HP. The default respawn delay is 12 seconds and can be changed in match settings. You respawn at your most recently captured base; if it is lost, another owned base is used.

If your team has no bases when you die, you cannot respawn until a teammate captures one. Recapturing a base revives waiting teammates. Base capture requires the base system to be ON.

The capture gauge is a remaining bar: it shrinks until capture succeeds. Standing on your damaged core without enemies restores it until the bar is full.

How do bases affect victory?

With the base victory condition selected, owning every base wins immediately. At the time limit, base count decides the winner, then territory count breaks a tie. Equal counts in both result in a draw.

With the base system ON, a team with no bases and no living players is eliminated. The match also ends when only one team remains.

What does owning a cannon do?

A captured cannon fires automatically for its team and paints eligible floor tiles, including obstacle floors. You do not need to stay there after capture. An opponent can take it back by completing the same five-second capture.

Its owned tile can help close an enclosure, but the cannon tile itself does not count toward territory. The handheld Plasma Cannon is a separate weapon.

Set up a local match

  1. Choose team count, team size and CPU settings on the first page. The displayed participant count is the total number of slots; local play has one human-controlled player.
  2. Use the page arrow to review match rules, including the map, items, cannons, match length and base system. With bases OFF, the victory condition is fixed to territory control.
  3. Choose Start Game to apply the settings and play. Cancel or Esc discards your edits and returns to the main menu.

Settings & controller

Settings has three pages: sound/display/name, controller controls, and controller greeting shortcuts. Controller support starts OFF; enable it on page two using the keyboard and mouse and select a connected controller.

Use the D-pad or left stick to navigate the UI. Confirm opens a dropdown; up/down selects an option and confirm applies it. For sliders, confirm starts adjustment, left/right changes the value, and confirm or back finishes. In presets A/B, the south face button confirms and the east button goes back; C/D reverse them. Text entry uses the keyboard.

Get ready together

From lobby to match

Check the room rules, choose a team and mark yourself ready. Online matches require at least two human players.

  1. Choose your team. Use W, A, S and D to move into its colored area, or use the random area for automatic assignment. Full teams and CPU-only teams cannot accept your ready choice.
  2. Ready up. Press F or select Ready (F). Your character stays in place while ready. Press F again or Unready (F) to change your choice.
  3. Wait for the host. When everyone is ready, the host confirms the start. OK is selected automatically after nine seconds. Keep Waiting makes the host unready again.
  4. Start together. Confirmation leads to team assignment and a five-second countdown. Guests cannot cancel readiness during the host’s start confirmation.

Before you try it

I painted around an area. Why did nothing happen?

Check that Enclosure is ON and that your line has no gaps. Every link must be your team’s color, including any cannon tile used in the boundary. Holes, base tiles and the edge of the map cannot close the gap for you.

Do I have to paint every tile in the loop myself?

No. The loop belongs to your team. Tiles painted by teammates, CPU teammates, cannon fire and earlier enclosures all count.

Does a closed loop protect its tiles forever?

No. Enclosure captures tiles when a new loop closes. Opponents can take the tiles back; an existing loop does not continuously repaint its interior.

Will enclosing a base capture it?

No. Bases and cannons use their own capture rules. An enclosure only takes eligible floor tiles.
